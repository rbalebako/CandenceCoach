This 4-week progressive plan introduces cadence work and form drills in small, manageable doses, blending them into your regular runs.
Week 1: Awareness and Baseline
Goal: Establish your current rhythm and introduce short, low-stress cadence intervals.

Run 1 (Easy Run, 20–30 mins): Count your total steps (right foot only) for 60 seconds during the middle of your run, then multiply by 2 to find your baseline cadence (e.g., if you count 75, your cadence is 150 SPM). Just become aware of how it feels.
Run 2 (Cadence Micro-Intervals):
Warm up with 10 minutes of easy jogging.
Do 4 x 30-second pickups: Set a metronome or use a 170 BPM playlist/beat. Try to match your feet to the beat for just 30 seconds. Focus on light, quick steps, then jog normally for 90 seconds to recover. Repeat 4 times.
Form Drill (Post-Run): A-Skips (2 sets of 15 meters): March while rhythmically skipping, driving your knees up to hip height and landing softly on your midfoot. This trains quick ground contact.
Week 2: Building Neuromuscular Memory
Goal: Extend the duration of your cadence intervals and add posture work.

Run 1 (Easy Run, 25 mins): Incorporate 4 x 1-minute cadence intervals. Set your metronome to roughly 5–8 beats higher than your baseline (don't jump straight to 180 if you're at 150; build gradually). Jog easy for 2 minutes between each interval.
Run 2 (Fartlek Cadence Play): During a 30-minute run, use telephone poles or landmarks. Every time you see a landmark, pick up your leg turnover and run at your target cadence for 30 seconds before settling back down. Do this 6 times throughout the run.
Form Drill (Post-Run): High Knees / Fast Feet (2 sets of 20 seconds): Run in place focusing on a rapid turnover—getting your feet off the ground as quickly as possible rather than lifting them too high.
Week 3: Integrating Form and Posture
Goal: Combine quick leg turnover with proper upper-body and hip alignment.

Run 1 (Cadence Blocks):
10-minute easy warmup.
3 x 2-minute cadence blocks targeting 5–10 beats above your starting cadence. Focus on keeping your stride short and landing underneath your hips. Take 2 minutes of walking/easy jogging between blocks.
Run 2 (Hill Strides): Find a very gentle, short incline (a 3-to-4 percent grade is ideal). Jog up it for 15 seconds, focusing on driving your elbows back and keeping a tall posture. Walk back down. Repeat 4 times. Hills naturally prevent overstriding and force a higher cadence.
Form Drill (Post-Run): Butt Kicks (2 sets of 15 meters): Lightly jog forward while bringing your heels up toward your glutes, keeping your upper body tall and core engaged.
Week 4: Consolidation and New Normal
Goal: Weave your new cadence into a seamless, natural stride.

Run 1 (Continuous Metronome Segment):
Warm up for 10 minutes.
Turn on your metronome (or a 170–175 BPM track) and run continuously at that cadence for 5 straight minutes. Focus on relaxing your shoulders and breathing rhythmically. Walk/jog easy for 3 minutes, then repeat the 5-minute block one more time.
Run 2 (The "Effortless" Easy Run): Go for a 30-minute run where your only mental cue is "light and quick." Check your watch or step-counter periodically to see if your baseline cadence naturally sits higher than it did on Day 1.
